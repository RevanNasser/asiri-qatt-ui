# From Asir

«Marhaba alf» is a warm greeting at the start of a visit. We use it here only as a welcome, without claiming any particular historical meaning; accounts of its origin vary.

## Al-Qatt Al-Asiri

Al-Qatt Al-Asiri is an art of decorating interior walls, especially the rooms where guests are received. UNESCO describes it as a practice associated with women, passed between generations by watching and doing, and inscribed it on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2017.

## How we use inspiration

Qatt's artwork is original design by the project, drawing on a general geometric vocabulary; it does not copy any particular wall, artist's work or village. Use motif names as documented labels where sources exist, and say clearly what is design interpretation.

## From the wall to the interface

Ghaida Al-Omari's 2024 study offers a useful reading of qatt as visual structure: a quiet ground, bands that divide the space, then geometric units repeated with rhythm and symmetry. That is the logic Qatt learns from when building a hero, a divider or a pattern background — not a recipe for rebuilding a traditional wall.

Styles differ between villages and governorates, and the study does not establish an exact date for the art's origin. So we do not impose an order on motifs or meanings on colours, and we cite our sources next to any heritage context.
